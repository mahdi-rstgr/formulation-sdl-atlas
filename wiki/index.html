    Self-Driving Laboratories for Formulation¶

    How a self-driving lab doses liquids, pastes and powders, moves the containers that hold them, rehearses each step in a digital twin, and decides what to make next.

    Open the atlas Seven-step map Figures

    This wiki is a browsable, living companion to the review Self-Driving Laboratories for Formulation: Handling Liquids, Pastes, Powders and Objects, from Robots to Digital Twins and AI Agents. It follows the review's seven steps and includes its tables, all ten figures and 178 linked references.

    Formulation means combining ingredients into products whose properties depend on both composition and process. An autonomous formulation experiment is a physical choreography:

    1. ingredients with very different rheology are dosed into one premix container;
    2. the container is carried to a mixer;
    3. the mix is processed and characterized, often with materials the robot has never handled.

    Landmark SDLs show that this physical layer can limit autonomy as much as the planning algorithm does [3], [4]. The guiding question at every step is how should this material, in this container, be handled next?

    The seven steps¶

    1 Dose How is each ingredient dosed? Materials, dispensing, 20 scenarios, strategies

    2 Move How do containers move? Gantries, SCARA, arms, cobots, mobile manipulators

    3 Buy What can be bought? Dispensers and integrated platforms by accuracy and price

    4 Extend How to go beyond the catalog? Retrofits: end-effectors, sensors, control software

    5 Rehearse Will the plan work? Fidelity hierarchy, simulators, digital twins

    6 Orchestrate How do devices run as one? Layers L1–L4, interfaces, eight open-source systems

    7 Decide What to make next? Data, surrogates, Bayesian optimization, AI agents
